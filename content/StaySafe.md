## Staying Safe: A Guide for Everyone

A comprehensive safety guide covering physical, social, digital, and emergency scenarios.

The world is mostly a wonderful place full of good people with good intentions. But predators do exist, and they look for a victim. That's why it helps to know some common tactics they use - so you can protect yourself.

**Start with any section that matters most to you, or read through all of them to build comprehensive safety awareness.**

Don't enter abandoned buildings - they may have an owner, which can count as trespassing.

Don't share 18+ photos. If anyone in the photo is under 18, it can be a criminal offense (child sexual content).

Don't joke about bombs, fires, or terrorist attacks - especially in a crowded place or online. This can be a criminal offense.

Memorials, monuments, the eternal flame, any vandalism - and even gestures - can be a criminal offense. Monuments are under surveillance.

Don't agree to  "easy money" (carry, deliver, place, remove from a card, hide). This can be a criminal offense - and you could be putting your life at risk (for example, by delivering explosives).

If the police stop you, don't run, don't insult, and don't be rude. Ask for a call, wait for your parents, and don't tell anything else or sign anything without your parents or a lawyer. Even if there is a pressure, resist to say anything - it could be used against you. You have a right to remain silent by law.

Be careful with how you present your sexuality - attention can come from everyone (including dangerous people), so think about the consequences.

Exchanging ("laundering") money for a percentage is participating in money laundering.

Any stray animals - cats, dogs, squirrels - can carry rabies. If you're bitten, even slightly, go to a doctor right away.

Sports betting and similar gambling are never a smart bet - never. It's a brain trap: it remembers the wins and filters out the other nine losses.

Sex with someone under the legal age of consent in your state is punishable by law - even if they claim it’s consensual or that they’re older. Don’t trust blindly; people can lie about their age.

Don't fall for "dare you" challenges ("do it," jump, drink). It's a provocation that often ends badly - and people who provoke usually don't face the consequences and risk nothing.

Don’t do dangerous or stupid things, and never risk your health, life, or well-being for nothing. You won’t really impress anyone - even if they cheer, they’ll forget about it in five minutes. And if you fail, the same people will just call you a loser.

If someone approaches you with an "amazing opportunity", a super sale - treat it as a red flag. Don't go anywhere with them, and don't buy, sign, or share personal information until you research calmly on your own. Tell them politely you’re not interested - even if they insist - and then walk away and continue your day.

If an aggressive person approaches you, record what’s happening (if possible and safe), ask them to leave you alone, and call security/cops. Don’t yell back or try to "solve" it yourself. Be smarter than that.

Avoid drunk or unstable people. Ignore them and try not to make eye contact.

Drugs - you can't "try once and forget." They quickly lead to addiction and damage the natural systems that create pleasure and joy.

Social
You don’t need a fancy restaurant to date someone. In fact, it’s often better to live an active social life - go to events, meet people, and spend time together in public places. These days, romantic relationships can be exploited by someone trying to take advantage of you. If someone is trying to lure you to a bar or restaurant, treat it as a potential trap. Be cautious, and never feel bad about saying no.

Gangs may try to provoke you or set up fake arguments - especially where people are drinking. If you get pulled into it and go outside to "talk", expect an organized group to attack you from behind and steal your belongings and disappear while you may be seen as the aggressor. Be careful. When possible, de-escalate and stay in public, safe areas.

Watch your food and drinks. Don’t leave them unattended as you never know who can add what to your food/drink and why.

In most countries, restaurants don’t offer free refills. Ordering a Coke/water can cost more than a meal.

Online
Most "free" software (games, VPN apps, "antivirus") bundled with malware which can waste your computer’s resources (e.g., cryptocurrency mining or joining botnets for coordinated attacks) and can also spy on you or steal data - personal emails/messages, bank accounts/money used online or monitor your online activity to blackmail you.

When possible, use the internet to schedule an offline meeting instead of discussing everything online. It’s easy to impersonate people there - don’t trust blindly; verify, call to make sure, use common sense.

Don't talk/chat to unknown contacts - even if they claim to be from government offices, including "from school or bank." This is fraud.
Social media is a public space: calls, likes, sharing extremist content, and fake/false information can be punishable.

Don't click links from unknown messages (DMs, texts, "emails," or social media chats), especially if they create urgency ("act now," "your account will be blocked").

Don't enter passwords, card details, or codes (2FA/OTP) when someone asks for them in chat - legitimate services won't request that way.

Verify the sender before trusting requests: check the account handle carefully, look for impersonation (extra letters, look‑alike names), and confirm via an official website or the official app.

Turn on strong account security: use a unique password for each account and enable two‑factor authentication (authenticator app or security key if available).

Be suspicious of "attachment" messages (PDFs, APKs, "documents," "support files") or "log in to view" content - avoid opening them from unknown sources.

If you think you've been phished, stop interacting immediately, log out/change passwords from a trusted device, and report/block the account.
