## 💻 Online Safety

Protecting yourself in digital spaces and preventing cyber attacks.

### Malware & Software Security

Most "free" software (games, VPN apps, "antivirus") bundled with malware which can waste your computer's resources (e.g., cryptocurrency mining or joining botnets for coordinated attacks) and can also spy on you or steal data - personal emails/messages, bank accounts/money used online or monitor your online activity to blackmail you.

### Meeting People Online

When possible, use the internet to schedule an offline meeting instead of discussing everything online. It's easy to impersonate people there - don't trust blindly; verify, call to make sure, use common sense.

### Avoiding Fraud & Scams

Don't talk/chat to unknown contacts - even if they claim to be from government offices, including "from school or bank." This is fraud.

Don't click links from unknown messages (DMs, texts, "emails," or social media chats), especially if they create urgency ("act now," "your account will be blocked").

Don't enter passwords, card details, or codes (2FA/OTP) when someone asks for them in chat - legitimate services won't request that way.

Verify the sender before trusting requests: check the account handle carefully, look for impersonation (extra letters, look‑alike names), and confirm via an official website or the official app.

Be suspicious of "attachment" messages (PDFs, APKs, "documents," "support files") or "log in to view" content - avoid opening them from unknown sources.

### Phishing & Account Compromise

If you think you've been phished, stop interacting immediately, log out/change passwords from a trusted device, and report/block the account.

### Account Security

Turn on strong account security: use a unique password for each account and enable two‑factor authentication (authenticator app or security key if available).

### Social Media Awareness

Social media is a public space: calls, likes, sharing extremist content, and fake/false information can be punishable.
