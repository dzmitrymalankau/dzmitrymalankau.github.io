Staying safe in public spaces and physical environments.

> ⚠️ **Key Takeaways**
> - Trust your instincts about people and situations
> - Understand legal boundaries—what seems harmless can have serious consequences
> - Know that most people are trustworthy; these tips help you spot the exceptions

## Physical Safety & Belongings

Don't pick up other people's belongings - these could be stolen items, weapons, explosives, or drugs.

Don't pick up wallets. Scammers often claim there was money in the wallet.

Don't pass on other people's belongings. If someone asks you to hand something over or hold it (even a package), it may contain prohibited items - and you could be held responsible.

## Legal Awareness

Don't stand nearby if someone is committing an illegal act (stealing, breaking things, assault). Being there can make you an accomplice.

Don't break or vandalize things - this is property damage and can be a criminal offense.

Don't enter abandoned buildings - they may have an owner, which can count as trespassing.

Don't share 18+ photos. If anyone in the photo is under 18, it can be a criminal offense (child sexual content).

Don't joke about bombs, fires, or terrorist attacks - especially in a crowded place or online. This can be a criminal offense.

Memorials, monuments, the eternal flame, any vandalism - and even gestures - can be a criminal offense. Monuments are under surveillance.

Don't agree to "easy money" (carry, deliver, place, remove from a card, hide). This can be a criminal offense - and you could be putting your life at risk (for example, by delivering explosives).

Exchanging ("laundering") money for a percentage is participating in money laundering.

## Handling Dangerous Situations

If an aggressive person approaches you, record what's happening (if possible and safe), ask them to leave you alone, and call security/cops. Don't yell back or try to "solve" it yourself. Be smarter than that.

Avoid drunk or unstable people. Ignore them and try not to make eye contact.

## Personal Presentation & Risk Management

Be careful with how you present your sexuality - attention can come from everyone (including dangerous people), so think about the consequences.

Don't fall for "dare you" challenges ("do it," jump, drink). It's a provocation that often ends badly - and people who provoke usually don't face the consequences and risk nothing.

Don't do dangerous or stupid things, and never risk your health, life, or well-being for nothing. You won't really impress anyone - even if they cheer, they'll forget about it in five minutes. And if you fail, the same people will just call you a loser.

## Recognizing Scams & Traps

If someone approaches you with an "amazing opportunity", a super sale - treat it as a red flag. Don't go anywhere with them, and don't buy, sign, or share personal information until you research calmly on your own. Tell them politely you're not interested - even if they insist - and then walk away and continue your day.

## Health & Animal Safety

Any stray animals - cats, dogs, squirrels - can carry rabies. If you're bitten, even slightly, go to a doctor right away.

## Substance Awareness

Drugs - you can't "try once and forget." They quickly lead to addiction and damage the natural systems that create pleasure and joy.

Sports betting and similar gambling are never a smart bet - never. It's a brain trap: it remembers the wins and filters out the other nine losses.

## Age of Consent & Exploitation

Sex with someone under the legal age of consent in your state is punishable by law - even if they claim it's consensual or that they're older. Don't trust blindly; people can lie about their age.
